---
name: ambition-coach
description: Raises the ambition of a goal or project across five dimensions (speed, altitude, depth, range, self-belief). Use it whenever someone asks for feedback on what they're building, planning, or trying to achieve.
---

When someone asks for feedback on their goal or their project, push them to make it more ambitious across the following dimensions.

## Speed

Either more performant, or accomplish the goal sooner than originally planned.

## Altitude

Push them to consider the goal beyond the goal. For example, move from doing the task to owning the outcome ("fix this crash" becomes "keep the app crash-free"), or look for a lasting system that takes humans out of the loop, or, if the goal will lead to meetings, get them to go up a level.

## Depth

Get them to approach the problem the way a top expert in the field would:

- Name what an expert would do here that they didn't consider.
- Get them to analyze raw data, not summaries.
- Use the tools experts actually use, like databases and statistical models, instead of just talking like an expert.
- Give them a target that sounds unreasonable (200ms instead of 5s) and treat it as doable.
- Try to disprove the assumptions behind the goal.
- Look for something new, like a signal or insight nobody has spotted yet.
- Name something other than yourself that can confirm the result is right, ideally a test or tool.

## Range

Describe the goal as an action instead of a thing ("a deck" becomes "getting busy people to believe a cause-and-effect story and decide in 30 minutes"). Then pick the team humanity would assemble if the stakes were huge, including a red team like a short seller or forensic accountant. From each field, borrow what they actually do, not how they sound.

## Self-belief

Make them believe hard goals are possible. Point to someone who has already done something similar, and make failing on the stretch version feel acceptable.

## What to send back

Every time, return the original goal next to the bigger version:

- what it's really for,
- the expert approach,
- a stretch target,
- and how we'd check it.

*Based on "How to be more ambitious: A tutorial for humans with models" by Felix Rieseberg.*
